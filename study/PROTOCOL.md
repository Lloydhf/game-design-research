# Proposed pilot protocol v0.1

**Not executed or preregistered.** Freeze this protocol and record the build commit before recruitment. Any later changes must be dated and explained.

## Question and design

Does presenting the same instruction at the point of use change target completion within 300 seconds, compared with presenting it before movement? Use a standalone, single-player parkour segment. Do not compare Roblox with Unity or change the geometry, controls, feedback, reset rules or target between groups.

- A: instruction shown immediately before movement begins.
- B: identical instruction shown in a predefined safe zone approaching the relevant obstacle.
- Fix the instruction text, font, display duration and dismissal behavior in both variants before testing. Both conditions receive identical baseline input-device instructions; only the additional mechanic explanation changes timing.
- Start the clock when the study condition first presents instruction or control. A's reading time is included. Stop at 300 seconds, participant withdrawal or target completion.
- One participant sees one condition, once. Do not reveal the hypothesis during the session.

## Recruitment and allocation

Target 12 consenting adults for a feasibility pilot, six per condition. This is not a powered confirmatory study. Randomly shuffle six A and six B assignments before the first session; keep the allocation list private. Collect a coarse prior-experience category and device category. Do not collect names, account IDs or exact ages. Record who actually enrolled, rather than filling missing rows to reach the target.

## Procedure

1. Explain the purpose, voluntary participation, the right to stop and what will be recorded. Obtain informed agreement; no recording by default.
2. Verify the same build, device configuration and target definition. Record a random session code and condition.
3. Ask the participant to reach the marked target. The observer gives no hints during the scored window. Record any accidental help.
4. Record duration, target reached and reset-causing failures. A reset is one failure; do not count every frame below a threshold.
5. Ask three separate 1–5 ratings: controls were clear; challenge felt fair; willingness to replay. Allow no answer. Do not treat these single items as validated psychological scales.
6. Optionally collect a short explanation of one confusing moment, then paraphrase without identifiers in private notes.

## Analysis and exclusions

The primary outcome is independent target completion within 300 seconds among consented, started sessions without technical invalidation. Early departure and help count as non-completion for this outcome, while being flagged separately. Do not replace early departures. Exclude a session only for a documented technical failure that prevents a valid exposure or for withdrawn data consent; report exclusion counts and reasons. Never keep rows after consent to use the data is withdrawn.

Report numerator/denominator and Wilson 95% intervals for each group. Report B minus A descriptively; the supplied tool does not calculate an interval or a significance test for the difference. Successful completion times are explicitly conditional on success and must not replace completion rate. Show missing responses and dropouts. Do not interpret overlapping or non-overlapping individual intervals as a formal between-group test.

No results are available yet. Stop at the preset recruitment target or record why recruitment ended early. Inspecting interim outcomes to decide when to stop is not part of this protocol.

## Interpretation and next iteration

This small convenience sample cannot establish long-term retention or general effects across ages, platforms and games. Record experience imbalance, experimenter effects, accessibility barriers and technical failures. Use the pilot to improve the procedure. A larger follow-up would need an explicitly chosen effect size, precision/power planning and a revised analysis plan.

Keep consent records and row-level data outside GitHub. Publish only suitable aggregate statistics with the scope agreed by participants. The repository currently contains no participant data.
